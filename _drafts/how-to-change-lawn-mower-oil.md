**Nobody taught me this.** A push mower's oil change is the easiest engine maintenance there is,
and skipping it is the fastest way to kill the mower. Here's how to do it without spilling gas.

## Step-by-step

1. **Find your engine model** (stamped on the engine) and look up its oil type and capacity in the
   operator's manual.

   ![Close-up of a walk-behind lawn mower's small engine]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-change-lawn-mower-oil/step01-mower-engine.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Shixart1985](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vintage_lawn_mower_motor_engine_closeup.jpg), [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/), via Wikimedia Commons; resized.</small>

2. **Run the mower a few minutes** to warm the oil so it drains better, then shut it off and let
   the muffler cool enough to handle.
3. **Disconnect the spark plug wire** and tuck it away from the plug. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Hand pulling the rubber boot straight off the spark plug, wire tucked away from the plug.{% endcomment %}
4. **Deal with the gas.** Run the tank nearly empty beforehand, or remove the gas cap, put a plastic
   sandwich bag over the opening, and screw the cap back on over it. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Sandwich bag laid over the open gas tank neck with the cap screwed back on over it.{% endcomment %}
5. **Clean around the dipstick and drain plug** so dirt doesn't fall into the engine.
6. **Drain the oil** one of three ways:
   - **Extractor:** pump it out through the dipstick tube with an
     {% include aff-link.html text="oil extractor" url="AFFILIATE_LINK_PLACEHOLDER" retailer="Amazon" %}.
   - **Drain plug:** tip the mower (air filter side up) over a drain pan, remove the plug, and let it drain.
   - **No plug:** remove the dipstick and tip the mower (air filter side up) to pour the oil out of
     the fill tube into a pan. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Mower tipped with the air-filter/spark-plug side up, oil draining from the fill tube (or drain plug) into a pan; second angle showing the tipping direction clearly.{% endcomment %}
7. **Reinstall the drain plug** (if you removed one) and set the mower level on the ground.
   **Check the air filter for oil** after tipping. If it's oil-soaked, clean or replace it per the
   manual before you start the engine.
8. **Add new oil slowly** through the fill tube with a funnel. Stop short of the full capacity. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Funnel in the fill tube, small bottle of SAE 30/10W-30, pouring in a thin stream.{% endcomment %}
9. **Check the dipstick** the way your manual says (some are screwed in, some just rest on the tube).
   Add a little at a time until it reaches the full mark. Don't overfill.

   ![Walk-behind mower engine with its yellow-capped oil fill tube and dipstick visible at the front]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-change-lawn-mower-oil/step09-mower-engine-dipstick-cap.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Shixart1985](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Man_starting_a_lawn_mower_in_a_green_garden.jpg), [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/), via Wikimedia Commons; resized.</small>

10. **Replace the dipstick,** remove the sandwich bag from the gas tank, and reconnect the spark plug wire.
11. **Start it and check for leaks.** A little smoke at first can be spilled oil burning off.
12. **Recycle the old oil** in a sealed container. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Sealed jug labeled USED OIL next to the drain pan, ready to take to a parts store.{% endcomment %}

## Common mistakes

- Tipping the mower carburetor-side down and flooding the air filter
- Leaving the spark plug wire connected
- Pouring in the whole bottle and overfilling
- Forgetting the sandwich bag and leaking gas everywhere
