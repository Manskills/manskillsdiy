**Nobody taught me this.** Low tire pressure wears tires out early and hurts braking. Checking it
takes ten minutes; rotating your tires takes about an hour with the right gear.

## Part 1: Check tire pressure

1. **Check with cold tires.** First thing in the morning, or after the car has sat 3+ hours.
2. **Find your target pressure** on the placard on the driver's door jamb (or the owner's manual).
   Front and rear may be different. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Driver's door open, close-up of the Tire and Loading Information placard on the B-pillar/door jamb with the cold-pressure line readable (blur VIN if present).{% endcomment %}
3. **Remove the valve cap** from the first tire.

   ![Tire valve stem on an alloy wheel with the cap removed]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-check-tire-pressure-and-rotate-tires/p1-step03-valve-stem-cap-off.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Christopher Ziemnowicz (CZmarlin)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tire_valve_stem-cap_off.jpg), public domain, via Wikimedia Commons.</small>

4. **Press the {% include aff-link.html text="tire gauge" url="AFFILIATE_LINK_PLACEHOLDER" retailer="Amazon" %}
   straight onto the valve** until the hissing stops, and read it.

   ![Hands pressing a dial tire gauge onto a tire's valve stem]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-check-tire-pressure-and-rotate-tires/p1-step04-pressing-gauge-on-valve.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [U.S. Air Force photo by Airman Frank Snider](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tire_pressure_gauge.jpg), public domain, via Wikimedia Commons.</small>

5. **Add air if low,** in short bursts, rechecking as you go. If high (and the tires are truly cold),
   press the valve's center pin briefly to let some out. Never let air out of hot tires. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Gas-station or portable compressor chuck pressed on the valve stem, gauge readable.{% endcomment %}
6. **Replace the valve cap.** Repeat on all four tires, and the spare.
7. **Do the penny test.** Put a penny upside down in a tread groove. If you can see all of Lincoln's
   head, the tire is worn out. Check a few spots across each tire. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Macro: penny inserted head-down in a tread groove, one shot where all of Lincoln's head is visible (worn) and one where the top of his head is covered (OK).{% endcomment %}

   ![A simple tread depth gauge for car tires]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-check-tire-pressure-and-rotate-tires/p1-step07-tread-depth-gauge.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Simon Speed](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:TyreDepthGauge.JPG), [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/), via Wikimedia Commons.</small>

## Part 2: Rotate the tires

1. **Look up your rotation pattern** in the owner's manual. If your tires are directional (arrow on
   the sidewall) or the front and rear are different sizes, they can't be crossed side to side.

   ![Front wheel of a car with a directional tire; the rotation arrow on the sidewall shows rolling direction]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-check-tire-pressure-and-rotate-tires/p2-step01-directional-tire-arrow.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [PtrQs](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:20170312_TyreRotationDirection_VW_RLine_DSCN6983_PtrQs.jpg), [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/), via Wikimedia Commons; resized.</small>

2. **Park on flat, solid pavement.** Parking brake on, wheels chocked.
3. **Mark each tire's current position** with chalk (LF, RF, LR, RR). {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Chalk/crayon 'LF' written on the tire sidewall, close-up.{% endcomment %}
4. **Break the lug nuts loose** about a half turn with the tires still on the ground. Don't remove them.

   ![Man using a lug wrench to loosen lug nuts on a car wheel with the tire on the ground]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-check-tire-pressure-and-rotate-tires/p2-step04-breaking-lug-nuts-loose.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Wilfredor](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cambiando_llanta.jpg), [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/), via Wikimedia Commons; resized.</small>

5. **Lift the car at the manufacturer's lift point** and set it on jack stands. Shake it gently to
   make sure it's stable. If you have four stands, you can lift the whole car; with two, do one side
   at a time (front-to-rear swaps only) or follow your manual's procedure. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Floor jack at the manufacturer front/rear lift point on one side, two jack stands in place under the pinch welds, opposite side wheels chocked. Wide enough to see both stands.{% endcomment %}
6. **Remove the lug nuts and wheels** and move them to their new positions per the pattern.

   ![Car in a garage with one wheel removed, showing the bare hub and brake]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-check-tire-pressure-and-rotate-tires/p2-step06-wheel-removed.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Shixart1985](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Car_without_a_wheel_in_a_workshop._Portable_light_on_the_ground.jpg), [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/), via Wikimedia Commons; resized.</small>

7. **Thread the lug nuts on by hand,** then snug them in a star (crisscross) pattern. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Wheel close-up with numbered overlay (1-5 or 1-4) showing star/crisscross tightening order; graphic overlay added in post.{% endcomment %}
8. **Lower the car** off the stands.
9. **Torque the lug nuts** in a star pattern to the spec in your owner's manual using a
   {% include aff-link.html text="torque wrench" url="AFFILIATE_LINK_PLACEHOLDER" retailer="Amazon" %}.

   ![Click-type torque wrench with a socket fitted]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-check-tire-pressure-and-rotate-tires/p2-step09-click-torque-wrench.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Lead holder](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Torque_wrench_20-220_Nm.jpg), [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/), via Wikimedia Commons.</small>

10. **Reset the pressures** to the placard (front and rear may differ, and the tires just moved).
11. **Re-check lug nut torque** after the first 50–100 miles of driving.

## Common mistakes

- Filling tires to the sidewall maximum instead of the door placard
- Checking pressure right after a long drive
- Getting under a car on a jack with no stands
- Tightening lug nuts in a circle instead of a star pattern, or with an impact gun and no torque wrench
- Crossing directional tires side to side
