**Nobody taught me this.** Every house gets holes in the walls: nail holes, anchors that pulled
out, a doorknob that went through. Here's how to patch each size so it disappears under paint.

## Step-by-step

### Before you start (all sizes)

1. **Measure the hole** across its widest point and pick the method from Quick specs.
2. **Prep the area.** Drop cloth down. Cut away loose paper and crumbled gypsum with a utility knife
   and lightly sand the edges smooth. Wipe off the dust.

### Small holes (nail and screw holes)

1. **Overfill with spackle** using a putty knife; it shrinks as it dries.

   ![A flat putty knife with a straight steel blade]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-patch-a-hole-in-drywall/small-step01-putty-knife.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [HomeSpot HQ](http://www.homespothq.com/) ([source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Putty_Knife_(8559730739).jpg)), [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/), via Wikimedia Commons; resized.</small>

2. **Let it dry, sand lightly** flush, and recoat if it dipped. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Sanding sponge flattening a dried spackle spot; raking side light to show the surface is flush.{% endcomment %}

### Medium holes (about 1/2 to 6 inches)

1. **Apply the patch.** Mesh for smaller holes, overlapping at least an inch all around; a metal
   patch for doorknob-size holes. Press it flat. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Self-adhesive fiberglass mesh (or metal patch) pressed over a ~3 in hole, overlapping at least 1 in all round; ruler in frame.{% endcomment %}
2. **Spread a thin coat of compound** over the patch with a 6-inch
   drywall knife<!-- AFF:amazon:drywall-knife-6-inch -->, pushing it through the mesh.

   ![Ceiling drywall with blotches of joint compound over screw holes and taped seams]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-patch-a-hole-in-drywall/medium-step02-joint-compound-coat.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Tomwsulcer](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kitchen_renovation_spackling_to_cover_holes_and_tape_between_sheetrock_boards.JPG), [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/), via Wikimedia Commons.</small>

3. **Let it dry fully,** sand lightly, and apply a second thin coat, wider than the first.
4. **Add a third coat if needed,** using a 10–12 inch knife loaded from a mud pan and feathering the
   edges out to roughly a foot square so there's no hump. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): 10-12 in taping knife feathering the final coat about 12 in wide; mud pan in hand.{% endcomment %}

### Large holes (over 6 inches)

1. **Check inside the wall** for wires and pipes, then cut the hole into a neat square or rectangle. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Hole squared up with a drywall saw, pencil layout lines visible; flashlight looking into the cavity for wires/pipes.{% endcomment %}
2. **Install backer boards.** Slip a 1x3 inside the wall along one edge and screw through the drywall
   into it. Repeat on the opposite edge.

   ![Square hole cut in drywall with a wood backer board screwed in behind it]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-patch-a-hole-in-drywall/large-step02-backer-board-in-hole.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Ken Dyck](https://www.flickr.com/photos/46126365@N00/291046187/), [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/), via Flickr.</small>

3. **Cut a drywall piece** the same thickness as your wall to fit (measure the edge of the hole;
   walls shared with an attached garage are at least 1/2 inch by code), add construction adhesive to the boards, and
   screw it in.
4. **Tape and mud the seams.** Thick-ish coat of compound on the seams, press paper tape into it,
   then two more thin coats after each dries.

### All sizes: finish

1. **Final sand** with a fine sanding sponge until you can't feel the edge.

   ![Worker in a dust mask sanding drywall compound with a pole sander]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-patch-a-hole-in-drywall/finish-step01-sanding-drywall.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Georgia National Guard](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sanding_Drywall_%285891937910%29.jpg), [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/), via Wikimedia Commons.</small>

2. **Prime the patch,** then paint. Two thin coats of touch-up paint blend better than one heavy one. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Patched area with primer rolled on, ready for paint, beside an unprimed area for comparison.{% endcomment %}

## Common mistakes

- One thick coat of compound that cracks and needs a ton of sanding
- Not feathering wide enough, leaving a visible hump
- Skipping primer, so the patch "flashes" through the paint
- Cutting into a wall without checking for wires first
