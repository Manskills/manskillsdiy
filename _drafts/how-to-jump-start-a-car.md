**Nobody taught me this.** A dead battery is the most common way to get stranded, and jump
starting a car takes about 15 minutes once you know the clamp order. Here's the whole thing.

## Step-by-step

1. **Check the owner's manual first.** Some cars have the battery in the trunk and remote jump
   terminals under the hood. Hybrids and EVs often have special rules.
2. **Park the donor car close, but not touching.** Nose to nose or side by side, close enough for
   the cables to reach. Both cars: in Park (or Neutral with the parking brake on), ignition off,
   lights and accessories off.

   ![A set of red and black jumper cables with copper-jawed clamps]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-jump-start-a-car/step02-jumper-cables.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Qurren](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Booster_cables.jpg), [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/), via Wikimedia Commons; resized.</small>

3. **Find the terminals.** Open both hoods. Positive is marked **+** and usually has a red cover;
   negative is marked **−**. If the posts are crusty white or blue, scrub them with a wire brush. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Top-down shot of a typical 12 V battery with the red (+) cover flipped open and the (−) terminal clearly visible; add text labels in post. Clean terminals, gloves on hand in frame.{% endcomment %}
4. **Clamp red to the dead battery's positive (+).** Make sure the teeth bite clean metal.

   ![Red jumper cable clamp attached to the positive terminal of a car battery]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-jump-start-a-car/step04-red-clamp-on-positive.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Wtshymanski (English Wikipedia)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:CrocodileClamponBattery.jpg), public domain, via Wikimedia Commons.</small>

5. **Clamp the other red to the good battery's positive (+).**
6. **Clamp black to the good battery's negative (−).** {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Donor car battery with red clamp on (+) and black clamp on (−), both clamps fully biting metal.{% endcomment %}
7. **Clamp the last black to bare, unpainted metal on the dead car,** like a thick bolt on the
   engine block, away from the battery. Not the dead battery's negative post. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Dead car's engine bay: black clamp on a thick unpainted bolt/bracket on the engine block, at least 18 in from the battery, with the battery visible in the background for distance context.{% endcomment %}
8. **Start the donor car** and let it run 3–5 minutes.
9. **Try to start the dead car.** Crank for 5–10 seconds at most, then stop and rest a minute or two so
   the starter doesn't overheat. If it
   doesn't start, let the donor run another five minutes and try again.
10. **Once it's running, leave both cars running a few minutes** with the cables still on.
11. **Disconnect in reverse order:** black from the ground point, black from the good car, red from
    the good car, red from the car you just started. Don't let clamps touch. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Sequence of 4 small photos (numbered 1-4) showing each clamp coming off in reverse order; can be combined into one graphic.{% endcomment %}
12. **Drive it 20–30 minutes** without stopping, with AC and rear defrost off, so the alternator can
    recharge the battery.
13. **Get the battery tested.** A jump only gets you going. Most auto parts stores can test the
    battery and charging system.

   ![Mechanic in gloves checking a car battery with a meter under the hood]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-jump-start-a-car/step13-battery-voltage-check.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Shixart1985](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Auto_mechanic_checking_old_car_battery_voltage.jpg), [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/), via Wikimedia Commons; resized.</small>

## Common mistakes

- Putting the last black clamp on the dead battery instead of bare metal
- Mixing up positive and negative (red always goes to +)
- Clamping onto painted metal or corroded posts, so nothing flows
- Shutting the car off right after it starts and ending up dead again
