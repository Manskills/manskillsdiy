**Nobody taught me this.** Checking your oil takes five minutes and is one of the cheapest ways
to protect an engine. Here's how to read the dipstick and top off without making a mess.

## Step-by-step

1. **Park on level ground and shut the engine off.** If you just drove, wait a few minutes so the
   oil drains back into the pan.
2. **Pop the hood** and prop it securely.
3. **Find the dipstick.** It usually has a yellow or orange ring or loop. If you can't find it,
   check the owner's manual (some newer cars don't have one).

   ![Bright orange loop handle of an engine oil dipstick in an engine bay]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-check-and-add-engine-oil/step03-orange-dipstick-handle.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Frettie](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Oil_dipstick_in_%C5%A0koda_Fabia_I,_engine_1,2_HTP.jpg), [CC BY 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/), via Wikimedia Commons; resized.</small>

4. **Pull it out and wipe it clean** with a rag.

   ![Hand wiping an engine oil dipstick clean with a rag over the engine]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-check-and-add-engine-oil/step04-wiping-dipstick-with-rag.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Milchstraßenräuber](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Oelstand.jpg), [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/), via Wikimedia Commons.</small>

5. **Push it all the way back in,** wait a moment, and pull it out again.
6. **Read the level.** Look at where the oil film stops between the two marks (MIN/MAX, L/H, ADD/FULL,
   or two holes). In the middle or near the top is fine. At or below the low mark means add oil.

   ![Dipstick laid on a blue shop towel showing where the oil film stops between the level marks]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-check-and-add-engine-oil/step06-reading-dipstick-level.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Dvortygirl](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Engine_oil_dipstick_reading_level.JPG), [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/), via Wikimedia Commons.</small>

7. **Check the color.** Amber to dark brown is normal. Milky, foamy, or gritty oil means stop and
   have a mechanic look at it. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Three dipsticks or three drops on white paper towel side-by-side: fresh amber, normal dark brown, and (staged with a label, or from a junk engine) milky/foamy. Even daylight, white background.{% endcomment %}
8. **Put the dipstick back** fully seated.
9. **Find the oil filler cap** on top of the engine (oil-can symbol). Unscrew it.

   ![Engine oil filler cap on top of an engine cover]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-check-and-add-engine-oil/step09-oil-filler-cap.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Frettie](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cover_of_refill_oil_tank_in_1,2_HTP_engine_in_%C5%A0koda_Fabia_I.jpg), [CC BY 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/), via Wikimedia Commons; resized.</small>

10. **Add oil a little at a time** using a funnel<!-- AFF:amazon:funnel -->.
    Use the grade from your owner's manual. Start with about half a quart.

   ![Pouring motor oil through a yellow funnel into the oil filler opening]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-check-and-add-engine-oil/step10-adding-oil-with-funnel.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Dvortygirl](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Motor_oil_refill_with_funnel.JPG), [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/), via Wikimedia Commons; resized.</small>

11. **Wait a minute, then recheck** the dipstick. Repeat until it's near the full mark. Don't go over. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Over-the-shoulder shot: dipstick held horizontally next to the open filler, level at the FULL mark, filler cap visible off to the side.{% endcomment %}
12. **Reinstall the filler cap** tight and wipe up any drips.

## Common mistakes

- Checking on a slope, which gives a false reading
- Reading the dipstick without wiping it first
- Pouring oil into the wrong cap (coolant, brake fluid, or washer fluid)
- Dumping in a whole quart and overfilling
- Forgetting to put the filler cap back on
