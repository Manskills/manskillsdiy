**Nobody taught me this.** A slow bathroom sink is almost always hair stuck on the stopper or in
the trap. You can usually clear it in under an hour with no chemicals and no plumber.

## Step-by-step

1. **Clear out the cabinet** under the sink and put a bucket and towels down.
2. **Pull the stopper.** Some lift straight out or twist out. If it won't, look behind the drain pipe
   under the sink for a horizontal rod held by a nut (the pivot nut). Unscrew the nut, pull the rod
   back, and lift the stopper out from above. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Under-sink close-up of the pop-up's pivot rod and pivot nut on the back of the drain pipe, nut being loosened by hand; flashlight lit.{% endcomment %}
3. **Clean the stopper.** Expect a gross clump of hair and gunk. Scrub it with an old toothbrush. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Pulled stopper with the hair clump on it, held over a paper towel (gross but honest; keep it tight).{% endcomment %}
4. **Fish out the drain.** Push a hair clog tool<!-- AFF:amazon:hair-clog-tool -->
   down the drain, twist, and pull. Repeat until it comes up clean. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Plastic barbed hair-clog tool being pulled up out of the drain opening with debris on the barbs.{% endcomment %}
5. **Test with the stopper still out.** Run hot water. If it drains well, reinstall the stopper (put
   the rod back through the stopper's slot and tighten the pivot nut hand-tight) and you're done.
6. **Plunge if it's still slow.** Make sure the stopper is removed first; plunging with it in place
   won't clear the drain. Plug the overflow hole with a wet rag, put an inch or two of water
   in the sink, and give it several firm plunges with a small cup plunger.

   ![Plunger sealed over a sink drain]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-unclog-a-bathroom-sink/step06-plunging-a-sink.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Kung Pao Cowboy](https://www.flickr.com/photos/66831265@N07/6083515280/), [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/), via Flickr.</small>

7. **Open the P-trap if needed.** Bucket under the U-shaped pipe. Loosen both slip nuts by hand (or
   with channel-lock pliers), and drop the trap into the bucket.

   ![White PVC P-trap under a bathroom sink]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-unclog-a-bathroom-sink/step07-p-trap-under-sink.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Tony Webster](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Leaking_PVC_Pipe_Under_Sink_-_P_Trap_%2852842956605%29.jpg), [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/), via Wikimedia Commons; resized.</small>

8. **Clean the trap** and look into the pipe going into the wall. Clear anything you can reach.
9. **Snake the wall pipe if needed.** Feed a hand drain snake<!-- AFF:amazon:hand-drain-snake -->
   into the wall pipe, crank it until you feel the clog, work it, and pull it back out.

   ![Hand drain snake: coiled steel cable with a red crank handle]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-unclog-a-bathroom-sink/step09-hand-drain-snake.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Da loewe](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rohrreinigungsspirale-6mm-x-1,5m-mit-Spitze.jpg), [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/), via Wikimedia Commons; resized.</small>

10. **Reassemble the trap.** Washers seated with the tapered side toward the joint, nuts started by
    hand so they don't cross-thread, then hand-tight. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Close-up of a slip-joint washer on the tailpiece with the tapered side facing the joint, slip nut above it; second shot of nut hand-tightened.{% endcomment %}
11. **Reinstall the stopper** (rod through the slot, pivot nut hand-tight), then **run water for a
    minute** and check every joint for drips with a dry paper towel.

## Common mistakes

- Pouring chemical drain cleaner first, then opening the trap full of it
- Losing the pivot rod's small washer or clip
- Overtightening plastic slip nuts and cracking them
- Forgetting to plug the overflow, so plunging just pushes air out of it
