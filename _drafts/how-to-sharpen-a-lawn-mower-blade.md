**Nobody taught me this.** A dull blade tears grass instead of cutting it, which leaves brown,
ragged tips. Sharpening the blade takes under an hour and makes an old mower cut like new.

## Step-by-step

1. **Disconnect the spark plug wire** and tuck it away from the plug.
2. **Handle the gas.** Run the tank low first, or put a sandwich bag over the tank opening under
   the cap.
3. **Tip the mower on its side,** air filter and carburetor up.
4. **Mark the blade's bottom (grass side)** with a marker so it goes back on the right way. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Paint marker 'X' or 'DOWN' on the blade's grass side while it is still on the mower (mower tipped air-filter side up).{% endcomment %}
5. **Put on heavy work gloves,** then **block the blade** with a piece of wood wedged between the blade and the deck so it can't spin. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): 2x4 scrap wedged between blade and deck, gloved hand on the wrench pulling away from the cutting edge.{% endcomment %}
6. **Remove the blade bolt** with a socket wrench (usually counterclockwise; it may be very tight).
   Position the wrench so you pull it away from the cutting edge, not toward it. Keep any washers and
   adapters in order.

   ![Mower blade mounted under the deck, showing the center blade bolt and adapter]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-sharpen-a-lawn-mower-blade/step06-blade-bolt-and-adapter.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Tony Webster](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lawn_Mower_Blade_-_Grass_Clippings_%2817218096280%29.jpg), [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/), via Wikimedia Commons; resized.</small>

7. **Inspect the blade.** Bent, cracked, or worn thin? Replace it with an approved blade instead.
   Also check the blade adapter and its key (the small metal tab or pin that locks the adapter to
   the crankshaft). If either is cracked, bent, or sheared, replace it.
8. **Clamp the blade in a vise,** cutting edge up. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Blade clamped in a bench vise, cutting edge up, outer 3-4 in sticking out.{% endcomment %}
9. **Sharpen the cutting edge,** following the existing bevel. Use a
   {% include aff-link.html text="mill file" url="AFFILIATE_LINK_PLACEHOLDER" retailer="Amazon" %}
   pushed in one direction along the edge, or short light passes on a grinder. Focus on the outer
   3–4 inches. Take off nicks first. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Mill file stroking the cutting edge at the factory bevel angle, push stroke only; second macro of the bright new edge.{% endcomment %}

   ![Bench grinder with eye shields and tool rests on a workbench]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-sharpen-a-lawn-mower-blade/step09-bench-grinder.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Nemo5576](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Einhell_DSC_125_Pa191756_%28Nemo5576%29.jpg), [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/), via Wikimedia Commons; resized.</small>

10. **Do the other end** the same way, with about the same number of strokes.
11. **Balance it.** Hang the blade on a
    {% include aff-link.html text="cone balancer" url="AFFILIATE_LINK_PLACEHOLDER" retailer="Amazon" %}
    or the smooth shank of a 5/8-inch bolt held level. If one end drops, take a little more metal off
    that end (not the edge) and recheck until it sits level. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Blade hung on a cone balancer (or a 5/8 in bolt in a vise) sitting level; second shot showing one end dipping (unbalanced).{% endcomment %}
12. **Reinstall the blade** with the marked side down, all washers in their original order.
13. **Torque the blade bolt** to the spec in your operator's manual, with the wood block in place
    and gloves on, again pulling the wrench away from the cutting edge.
14. **Set the mower upright,** remove the bag from the gas tank, and reconnect the spark plug wire.
15. **Test run.** Heavy vibration means stop: shut it off, disconnect the plug wire, and recheck balance and installation.

## Common mistakes

- Turning the blade by hand with the plug wire still connected
- Putting the blade back upside down
- Skipping the balance check
- Grinding until the edge turns blue (overheated and softened)
