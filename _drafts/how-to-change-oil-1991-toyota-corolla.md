**Nobody taught me this.** If nobody taught you either, here's how to change the
oil on a 1991 Toyota Corolla yourself, start to finish, in about an hour.

> **Heads up: specs depend on your engine.** The 1991 Corolla was sold with more
> than one engine. Most US sedans and wagons have the 1.6L **4A-FE**; the GT-S
> coupe has the 1.6L **4A-GE**. Oil capacity and filter part number can differ
> between them. Check your owner's manual or the emissions label under the hood
> before you buy parts.

## Safety first

- **Never get under a car held up only by a jack.** Jacks can slip or fail. Always
  set the car down on jack stands placed at the manufacturer's lift points, on
  flat, solid ground, or use ramps.
- **Chock the wheels** that stay on the ground, and set the parking brake.
- **Hot oil burns.** Running the engine for a few minutes makes the oil drain
  better, but oil and the exhaust can get hot enough to burn skin. Let the car
  sit until the oil is warm, not hot, and keep your arm out of the stream when
  the plug comes out.
- Wear safety glasses. Oil and grit fall straight down into your face.
- Used oil is toxic. Don't dump it; most auto parts stores take it for free.

## Step-by-step: changing the oil

1. **Warm it up (briefly).** Run the engine 2–3 minutes, then shut it off. Let
   it sit a few minutes so nothing is scorching hot.
2. **Park and secure the car.** Flat ground, parking brake on, wheels chocked.
3. **Lift the front end safely.** Jack the car at the proper front lift point and
   set it down on two jack stands (or drive up onto ramps). Shake the car gently
   to make sure it's stable before going underneath.
4. **Open the oil filler cap** on top of the engine. This lets the oil drain
   faster.
5. **Locate the drain plug** on the bottom of the oil pan (the lowest point of
   the engine's oil pan, not the transmission).
6. **Drain the oil.** Put the drain pan underneath, loosen the plug with the
   correct socket **[VERIFY: drain plug size]**, then unscrew the last few
   threads by hand and pull it away quickly. Let it drain until it slows to a drip.
7. **Reinstall the drain plug** with a new crush washer. Thread it by hand first
   to avoid cross-threading, then tighten to **25 ft-lb (34 N·m)**. Don't
   overtighten; the oil pan threads strip easily.
8. **Remove the old oil filter.** Move the drain pan under the filter and use
   your [oil filter wrench](AFFILIATE_LINK_PLACEHOLDER) to turn it
   counterclockwise. Expect more oil to spill out.
9. **Check that the old gasket came off** with the filter. If the old rubber
   ring is stuck to the engine, peel it off. A double gasket will leak badly.
10. **Prep the new filter.** Wipe the mounting surface clean. Rub a thin film of
    fresh oil on the new filter's rubber gasket.
11. **Install the new filter by hand.** Spin it on until the gasket touches, then
    tighten it the additional amount printed on the filter or its box (often
    about 3/4 turn). Hand-tight only; don't crank it with the wrench.
12. **Lower the car** off the jack stands.
13. **Add new oil.** Using the funnel, pour in slightly less than the full
    capacity **[VERIFY: oil capacity]**, then put the filler cap back on.
14. **Check for leaks.** Start the engine and let it idle for a minute. The oil
    pressure light should go off within a few seconds. Look underneath for drips
    at the drain plug and filter.
15. **Check the level.** Shut it off, wait a few minutes, then pull the dipstick,
    wipe it, reinsert it, and read it. Top off a little at a time until the level
    is at the full mark. Don't overfill.
16. **Clean up and recycle.** Pour the used oil into a sealed container and drop
    it (and the old filter) at an auto parts store or recycling center.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting to reinstall the drain plug before pouring in new oil
- Leaving the old filter gasket stuck to the engine
- Overtightening the drain plug or the filter
- Overfilling. Go by the dipstick, not just the bottle
