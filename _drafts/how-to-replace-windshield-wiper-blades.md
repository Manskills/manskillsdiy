**Nobody taught me this.** Bad wipers are a safety problem the first time it pours. Swapping them
takes ten minutes in a parking lot, and you don't need any tools on most cars.

## Step-by-step

1. **Get the right sizes.** Look up driver, passenger, and rear blade lengths (owner's manual or
   parts-store lookup), or measure the old blades.

   ![Macro view of a used wiper blade's rubber edge]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-replace-windshield-wiper-blades/step01-worn-wiper-blade-edge.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Jacek Rużyczka](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Used_Michelin_8019_windshield_wiper_blade_tip.jpg), [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/), via Wikimedia Commons; resized.</small>

2. **Turn the car off** with the wipers parked. If you have rain-sensing wipers, move the stalk
   fully to OFF (not AUTO) first.
3. **Lay a folded towel on the windshield** under the wiper arm. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Wiper arm lifted, folded towel lying on the glass right under the arm's hook end.{% endcomment %}
4. **Lift the wiper arm** away from the glass. Most stay up on their own; if yours is under the hood
   edge, check the manual for a service position.
5. **Find the release.** On a hook arm, there's a small tab or button where the blade meets the arm.

   ![Fingers pressing the release tab where a wiper blade clips onto the J-hook of the wiper arm]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-replace-windshield-wiper-blades/step05-hook-release-tab.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Phil Parker](https://www.flickr.com/photos/45131642@N00/4551705148/), [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/), via Flickr.</small>

6. **Press the tab and slide the blade down** toward the base of the arm, then off the hook.
   If it's stuck, a light tap usually frees it. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): Hands sliding the old blade down past the J-hook; the bare hook end of the arm clearly visible.{% endcomment %}
7. **Check the adapter on the new blade.** Most come with a hook adapter already installed; others
   include several. Use the one that matches your arm. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): New blade out of package on a workbench with its J-hook adapter shown close-up (and any spare adapters from the package).{% endcomment %}
8. **Slide the new blade onto the hook** until it clicks into place. Tug it to be sure it's locked. {% comment %}SHOT LIST (original photo needed): New blade being pulled up into the hook, hook seated in the adapter, hand giving the tug test.{% endcomment %}
9. **Lower the arm gently** onto the glass. Don't let it snap down.

   ![Both windshield wipers lowered and parked at the base of the windshield]({{ '/assets/images/how-to-replace-windshield-wiper-blades/step09-wipers-parked-on-glass.jpg' | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy"}
   <small class="photo-credit">Photo: [Wesha](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Windshield_Wiper_2.jpg), public domain, via Wikimedia Commons.</small>

10. **Repeat on the other side** (and the rear, if equipped).
11. **Test with washer fluid.** Watch for streaks, chatter, or a blade that lifts off the glass.

## Common mistakes

- Letting a bare arm snap back onto the windshield
- Buying one size for both sides when the car uses two lengths
- Not hearing the click, so the blade flies off on the highway
- Forgetting the rear wiper
